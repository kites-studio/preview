03 Why 'Metabolists'

architecture as a living organism.

Metabolism was a post-war Japanese architecture movement, launched with a manifesto at the 1960 World Design Conference in Tokyo by Kiyonori Kikutake, Kisho Kurokawa, Fumihiko Maki and others, mentored by Kenzo Tange.

Its name comes from the Japanese shinchintaisha, the replacement of old with new: buildings and cities that grow, adapt and renew like living things.

Its ideas reached Singapore through architects such as William Lim and Tay Kheng Soon, shaping landmarks like People's Park Complex and Golden Mile Complex.

Design Metabolists was founded in 1973, as the movement was at its height, and carries the same belief: good design keeps evolving with the people who use it.
